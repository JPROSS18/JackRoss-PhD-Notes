# Probability Space
A probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$.

The set of all possible outcomes are labelled $\omega \in \Omega$. 

These events are grouped into subsets of $\Omega$ called a family $\mathcal F$ .

A family $\mathcal F$ has the following properties: 

1. $\varnothing \in \mathcal F$ 
2. Closed under compliment 
3. Closed under countable unions
This makes $\mathcal F$ a $\sigma$-algebra and $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ a measurable space. 

If $\mathcal C$ is a collection of subsets of $\Omega$, then $\sigma(\mathcal C)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal C$. 

If $\Omega = \mathbb R^d$ and $\mathcal C$ is the set of all open sets, then $\sigma(\mathcal C) = \mathcal B$, the Borel sigma algebra. 

# Random Variable

**Real Valued:**
A real-valued function $X: \Omega \to \mathbb R$ is $\mathcal F$-measurable if 
$$
\{\omega \ | \ X(\omega) \leq a \}, \forall a \in \mathbb R
$$
This function is called a real-valued *random variable*. 


**Notes:**
A multivariable function is random variable if and only if each co-ordinate function is a random variable. 

The indicator function $I_A$ is a r.v. if and only if $A$ is $\mathcal F$-measurable. 

**General Setting:**

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and $(\Omega', \mathcal{F}')$ be two measurable spaces. A mapping $X: \Omega \to \Omega'$. is $(\mathcal F, \mathcal F')$-measurable if 
$$
\{\omega \ | \ X(\omega) \in A' \} \in \mathcal F, \forall A' \in \mathcal F'
$$
(The pre-image of every measurable set is measurable.)

$X$ is a $\Omega '$-valued $(\mathcal F, \mathcal F')$-measurable random variable. 

$X: \Omega \to \mathbb R ^d$ be any function. The $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma(X)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra on $\Omega$ containing all the sets $\{ \omega : X(\omega) \in U \}$, $U \subset \mathbb R^d$ open. 
$\sigma$-algebra generated by the pre-image all open sets. $\sigma (X)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra for which $X$ is a measurable function. 

**Lemma (Doob-Dynkin):**
If $X,Y: \Omega \to R^d$ are two given functions. Y is $\sigma(X)$-measurable if and only if $\exists \ g: \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^d$ that is Borel measurable with $Y = g(X)$. 






# Probability Measure

A *probability measure* $P$ on a measurable space $(\Omega, \mathcal F)$ is a function $P: \mathcal F \to [0, 1]$. 

1. $P(\Omega) = 1$
2. Disjoint sets have additivity. 

The triple $(\Omega, \mathcal F, P)$ is a probability space. 

Notion of Completeness in a $\sigma-$algebra 

$$
\bar{ \mathcal F } = \{ A \subset \Omega \ | \ \exists B,C \in \mathcal F \ s.t \ B \subset A \subset C, \ P(B) = P(C) \}
$$

Sets which are 'dense' in the $\sigma$-algebra. 

# Notes

*Expectation* of $X$:
$$
EX = \int_\Omega X(w) dP(w)
$$
*Variance* of $X$:
$$
V(X) = E(X - EX)^2
$$

*Covariance* of$X$ and $Y$: 
$$
Cov(X, Y) = E[(X - EX)(Y - EY)]
$$
for real-values random variables and 
$$
Cov(X, Y) = E[(X - EX)(Y - EY)^T]
$$
for $\mathbb R^d$-valued random variables. This is called the *covariance matrix*.

# Inequalities

There a several important inequalities for probability theory which mostly arise from functional analysis. 

**Holder's inequality:**
$$
|E(X^TY)| = (E|X|^p)^{1/p}(E|Y|^q)^{1/q}
$$
when $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$.
Generalisation of Cauchy-Swartz inequality. 

**Minkovski Inequality**
$$
(E(|X + Y|^p))^{1/p} \leq (E|X|^p)^{1/p} + (E|Y|^p)^{1/p}
$$
for $p > 1$. 

Generalisation of triangle inequality. 

**Chebyshev's Inequality:**
$$
P\{\omega : |X(w)| \geq c\} \leq c^{-p}E|X|^p
$$
# Types of Convergence

- Almost surely 
- In probability 
- pth moment convergence 
- Convergence in distribution. 

**Almost Surely:**

A sequence of random variables $\{ X_k\}$ converges almost surely to $X$ ($X_k \to X\ a.s.$ ) if 
$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall \omega \in \Omega \backslash \Omega_0, \quad P(\Omega_0) = 0, \quad \forall \epsilon >0 \\
\exists N \in \mathbb N \quad s.t \quad ||X_k(\omega) - X(w) || < \epsilon, \quad \forall k > N
\end{aligned}
$$
Pointwise convergence excluding sets of measure 0. 

**Convergence in Probability:**
A sequence of random variables $\{ X_k\}$ converges *in probability* to $X$ if:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall \epsilon > 0, P\{ \omega: ||X_k(\omega) - X(\omega) || > \epsilon\} \to 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Weaker that a.s convergence and $L^p$ convergence. 

**$L^p$ Convergence:**
A sequence of random variables $\{ X_k\}$ converges in *pth moment* to $X$ if:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E  |X_k(\omega) - X(\omega) |^p \to 0, \quad X_k, X \in L^p
\end{aligned}
$$

Convergence in the $pth$ moment implies convergence in lower moments. 

**Convergence in Distribution:**
A sequence of random variables $\{ X_k\}$ converges in *distribution* to $X$ if for every continuous and bounded function $g$:
$$
Eg(X_k) \to Eg(X)
$$
Weakest form of convergence, implies by convergence in probability



**Note**
$L^p$ convergence does not imply a.s convergence and vice versa. However the condition
$$
\sum^{\infty}_{k = 1}
E|X_k - X|^p < \infty$$
implies almost surely convergence (series convergence is stronger that $L^p$ convergence.). 


# Convergence Theorems

**Monotone Convergence Theorem:**
If $\{ X_k \}$ is an increasing sequence of non-negative random variables, then 
$$
\lim_{k \to \infty} EX_k = E(\lim_{k\to \infty}X_k)
$$

**Dominated Convergence Theorem:**
Let $p \geq 1$, $\{ X_k \} \subset L^p(\Omega; \mathbb R^d)$ and $Y \subset L^p(\Omega; \mathbb R^d)$. 

Assume that $|X_k| \leq Y$ a.s. and $\{ X_k \}$ converges to $X$ in probability.  

Then $X \subset L^p(\Omega; \mathbb R^d)$ and $X_k \to X$ in $L^p$. 

# Important Notation

A collection of sub-$\sigma$-algebras, $\{ \mathcal F_i : i \in I \}$, is said to be *independant* if for every possible choice $i_1, ... , i_k \in I$, 
$$
P( A_{i1} \ \cap .... \ \cap A_{ik}) = P(A_{i1}) \ ... \ P(A_{ik})
$$

A family of random variables $\{ X_i \ | \ i \in I  \}$ is said be *independant* if the $\sigma$-algebras, $\sigma (X_i), i \in I$, generated by them are independent. 

For example, two random variables, $X: \Omega \to \mathbb R^d, Y: \Omega \to \mathbb R^m$ are said to be independent if and only if: 
$$
P\{ \omega \ | \ X(\omega) \in A, Y(\omega)\in B\} = P\{ \omega \ | \ X(\omega) \in A \} P \{ \omega \ | \ Y(\omega)\in B\}
$$
holds for all $A \in \mathcal B^d, B \in \mathcal B^m$. 

If $X, Y$ are independent $XY$ is also integrable and 
$$
E(XY) = EX \  EY
$$
If $X, Y$ are *uncorrelated*, then 
$$
V(X+Y) = V(X) + V(Y)
$$
If the X and Y are independent, they are uncorrelated. 

If $(X, Y)$ has a normal distribution, then $X$ and $Y$ are independent if and only if they are uncorrelated. 

Let $\{ A_k \}$ be a sequence of sets $\mathcal F$. Define the *upper limit of the sets* by 
$$
\limsup_{k \to \infty} A_k = \{ \omega \ | \ \omega \in A_k \ \text{for infinitely many k} \} = \bigcap_{i =1}^{\infty} \bigcup_{k =1}^{\infty} A_k.
$$
**Borel-Cantelli's Lemma:**
1. If $\{ A_k \} \subset \mathcal F$ and $\sum^{\infty}_{k =1} P(A_k) < \infty$
Then:
$$
P(\limsup_{k \to \infty } A_k) = 0
$$

2.  If $\{ A_k \} \subset \mathcal F$ is independent and $\sum^{\infty}_{k =1} P(A_k) = \infty$
Then: 
$$
P(\limsup_{k \to \infty} A_k) = 1
$$


**Conditional Probability:**
$A, B \in \mathcal F$ with $P(B) > 0$. The *conditional probability* of *under condition* B is:
$$
P(A | B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
$$


**Conditional Expectation:**
A more general concept is needed when conditioning by a whole family. 

Let $X \in L^1(\Omega, \mathbb R)$ be a real-valued random variable and let $\mathcal G \subset \mathcal F$ be a sub-$\sigma$-algebra. 

We want a r.v. $Y$ which is $\mathcal G$-measurable such that 
$$
E(I_{G}Y) = E(I_{G}X), \quad \forall \ G \in \mathcal G.
$$
By the Radon-Nikodym theorem: 
There exists a $Y$ which is almost surely unique. This $Y$ is called the *conditional expectation* of $X$ under the condition $\mathcal G$. 
$$
Y = E(X | \mathcal G)
$$
If $\mathcal G$ is $\sigma(Y)$, we write:
$$
E(X | Y) = E(X | \mathcal G)
$$
